# Frailty Knowledge Cluster and Team 4 Main Plan Design

Date: 2026-07-31
Status: Ready for user review
Project: บ้านกายภาพ ชลบุรี Care Navigator

## 1. Purpose

Create a clinically grounded, marketing-ready foundation for the term
`ผู้สูงอายุเปราะบาง` and use that foundation to define one Team 4 main plan.
The app remains the primary destination for paid advertising. The four website
articles provide contextual education after the user starts the app and can
also receive organic search traffic independently.

The primary promise is:

> ช่วยผู้สูงอายุเปราะบางกลับมาช่วยเหลือตัวเองได้มากขึ้น

The content must help adult children and caregivers decide what can be managed
at home, what requires more information, and when paying for home physical
therapy is likely to add meaningful value.

## 2. Audience and Positioning

### Primary reader

- Adult children and caregivers who can understand basic health information.
- They are commonly the person comparing options and deciding whether to pay
  for care.

### Secondary reader

- Older adults who should be able to read the headings, summaries, and actions
  independently.
- More technical explanations may require help from a family member.

### Market category

Use the term `ผู้สูงอายุเปราะบาง` directly. Do not hide it behind generic
phrases such as `แรงตก` or `เดินช้าลง`, although those phrases should explain
what the term means in daily life.

The service is positioned for older adults who still have rehabilitation
potential: physical therapy may help them perform more activities by
themselves, use less assistance, or function more safely at home.

Do not claim that every person will recover. Do not position home care as
superior to hospital care in all circumstances. The differentiator is the
ability to assess and train meaningful activities in the person's real home.

## 3. Product Principles

1. Advertising brings a person into the app, but never determines their result.
2. Team 1 is identical for all users and is not highlighted by campaign source.
3. Campaign and article source may be recorded for aggregate analytics only.
4. Team 4 is computed from all answers, not selected directly in Team 1, 2, or 3.
5. Clinical answers, safety signals, function, home context, and recovery
   potential determine the result.
6. The result must place the person's problems before the service offer.
7. The app must explain when self-management is reasonable, when an assessment
   adds value, and what outcome would allow paid visits to stop.
8. The app is an educational screening and decision-support experience, not a
   diagnosis or a longitudinal medical record.

## 4. Team 1-4 Contract

### Team 1: Goal

Captures what the person wants to regain or protect. It contributes priority
weight but never selects a Team 4 plan by itself.

### Team 2: Conditions and lived problems

Captures known diagnoses, recent health events, and patient-facing problems.
It supplies clinical pathways and precautions but does not force a fixed plan.

### Team 3: Safety and current function

Captures red flags, assistance level, mobility, daily activities, endurance,
falls, home risks, caregiver availability, and relevant modifiers. A short
frailty signal screen can apply across all Team 1 goals, with deeper questions
shown only when indicated.

### Team 4: Aggregated result

Team 4 is composed from:

`goal + condition + current function + safety + home context + comorbidity + recovery potential`

Each result contains:

- one main plan profile;
- the three highest-priority problem blocks;
- no more than two supporting modifiers;
- a starter plan;
- optional quantitative assessments;
- a personalized FITT plan after enough information is completed;
- contextual knowledge links;
- transparent self-care, professional-assessment, and stop-point guidance.

## 5. Frailty Knowledge Cluster

The cluster contains exactly four articles. No separate frailty hub page is
required for the paid-ad journey. The normal blog index may list the articles.

### Article 1: ผู้สูงอายุเปราะบางคืออะไร?

Proposed slug: `frailty-in-older-adults`

Primary job: Build category understanding without making frailty sound like
normal ageing or a hopeless state.

Required sections:

1. Plain-language definition.
2. Difference between ageing, disease, disability, sarcopenia, and frailty.
3. Why a small stressor can cause a large decline.
4. Daily-life signs families may notice.
5. Frailty can change over time and should be assessed when the person is
   medically stable.
6. What to do next without self-diagnosing.

Primary contextual link in Team 4: the expandable `ผู้สูงอายุเปราะบาง`
condition entry.

App CTA in the article: `เริ่มประเมินจากแอพหลัก`.

### Article 2: ประเมินภาวะเปราะบางอย่างไร?

Proposed slug: `frailty-assessment`

Primary job: Explain the difference between noticing a signal, screening,
functional testing, and comprehensive assessment.

Required sections:

1. What families can observe before testing.
2. The five Fried phenotype domains in patient-safe language: unintentional
   weight loss, exhaustion, weakness, slow walking, and low activity.
3. Functional measures that may inform the plan: chair rise, gait speed,
   balance, walking ability, ADL, and IADL.
4. Why one test or one slow walking time does not confirm frailty.
5. When a comprehensive geriatric assessment or multidisciplinary review is
   appropriate.
6. Which tests are suitable for self-entry and which require a professional.

Primary contextual links in Team 4: each problem assessment panel and the
explanation of why the test changes the plan.

App CTA in the article: `กลับไปตอบแบบประเมินของฉัน`.

### Article 3: ผู้สูงอายุเปราะบางยังฟื้นตัวได้ไหม?

Proposed slug: `frailty-recovery-potential`

Primary job: Support an informed spending decision without guaranteeing
recovery.

Required sections:

1. Rehabilitation potential in plain language.
2. Why age alone cannot determine potential.
3. Positive signals: a recent decline from a higher baseline, ability to
   follow or participate, a meaningful goal, stable medical status, and family
   support.
4. Factors that may slow recovery or require another professional first.
5. What meaningful improvement looks like: less assistance, safer transfers,
   more independent household mobility, and greater activity tolerance.
6. How progress should be measured and when the plan should be revised.

Primary contextual link in Team 4: the opening summary and `เหตุผลที่ระบบเลือก
แผนนี้` explanation.

App CTA in the article: `ดูแผนและเป้าหมายของฉัน`.

### Article 4: ดูแลและฟื้นฟูผู้สูงอายุเปราะบางอย่างไร?

Proposed slug: `frailty-care-and-physical-therapy`

Primary job: Separate safe self-management from situations in which home
physical therapy or another professional may add value.

Required sections:

1. Person-centred goals and activity practice.
2. Progressive strength, balance, mobility, and endurance principles.
3. Nutrition, medication, cognition, mood, vision, hearing, and home hazards as
   important non-exercise contributors.
4. What a family can start and monitor safely.
5. What a physical therapist should assess at home.
6. When medical or multidisciplinary review should come first.
7. The paid-care stop point: the person and family can continue the plan safely,
   function is improving or stable, and professional reassessment is no longer
   changing management.

Primary contextual link in Team 4: personalized program, consultation decision,
and stop-point explanation.

App CTA in the article: `กลับไปดูสิ่งที่ทำเองได้และสิ่งที่ควรให้กายภาพช่วย`.

## 6. Team 4 Main Plan FRAIL-01

`FRAIL-01` is a provisional internal content ID. It does not replace or
renumber the existing patient-facing Plan 01 (`แผนเดินมั่นใจ ลดเสี่ยงล้ม`). The
final sequence number will be assigned only after the complete Team 4 plan
catalogue is reviewed.

### Patient-facing name

`แผนฟื้นกำลัง กลับมาช่วยเหลือตัวเอง`

### Profile label

`สำหรับผู้สูงอายุที่เริ่มมีสัญญาณเปราะบาง`

### Opening message

> ร่างกายยังมีความสามารถที่พัฒนาได้ เป้าหมายสำคัญตอนนี้คือเพิ่มแรงสำหรับการลุกยืน ทำให้การเดินปลอดภัยขึ้น และค่อย ๆ กลับไปทำกิจวัตรด้วยตัวเองได้มากกว่าเดิม

### Eligibility intent

Use this as the main profile when all of the following are true:

- no medical hard stop is active;
- the aggregate result contains frailty signals in multiple domains;
- the person has a goal related to maintaining or regaining independence;
- Team 1-3 information suggests meaningful function can still be trained;
- no single disease-specific or postoperative pathway has a higher immediate
  priority than the aggregate frailty pattern.

Frailty may still appear as a supporting modifier when another main plan, such
as Stroke or postoperative recovery, has higher priority.

## 7. Three Main Problem Blocks

### Problem 1: แรงลดลง ทำให้ลุกยืนและย้ายตัวลำบาก

Possible inputs:

- uses the arms to rise;
- requires supervision or physical assistance;
- bed, chair, toilet, or transfer performance has declined;
- strength declined after illness or reduced activity.

Self-entered assessment:

- 30-second chair stand count only when the prior safety answers permit it;
- assistance level: independent, uses arms, supervision, or physical help;
- no forced test when rising is unsafe.

Professional assessment:

- lower-limb and trunk strength;
- movement strategy and transfer assistance;
- bed, chair, and toilet height in the actual home.

### Problem 2: เดินช้าลงและเสียสมดุลง่าย

Possible inputs:

- furniture walking;
- fall or near-fall history;
- difficulty turning, walking at night, or reaching the toilet;
- mobility aid does not fit the home route.

Self-entered assessment:

- falls and near falls in the previous 30 days;
- safe household walking level;
- assistance level;
- a walking task only when Team 3 indicates that self-testing is safe.

Professional assessment:

- gait speed and gait pattern;
- balance, turning, and balance-recovery response;
- walking aid, footwear, and home hazards.

### Problem 3: ความทนทานลดลง ทำกิจวัตรได้ไม่ต่อเนื่อง

Possible inputs:

- frequent rest during daily activity;
- increased sitting or bed time;
- fatigue during bathing, dressing, or toileting;
- low intake, weight loss, or slow recovery.

Self-entered assessment:

- exertion or fatigue from 0-10 after a meaningful activity;
- continuous activity duration;
- recovery time;
- Barthel assessment only when Team 3 identifies reduced self-care ability.

Professional or multidisciplinary assessment:

- activity tolerance, pulse, blood pressure, and positional symptoms;
- comorbidities and medicines affecting exercise;
- nutrition or sarcopenia risk requiring referral or shared management.

## 8. Supporting Modifiers

Show at most two modifiers, selected by relevance and actionability:

- sarcopenia or nutrition risk;
- cognition or communication;
- osteoporosis and injury consequence;
- cardiovascular, respiratory, metabolic, or renal precautions;
- home environment and caregiver capacity;
- disease-specific or postoperative precautions.

Modifiers alter safety, exercise selection, FITT, monitoring, and referral. They
do not replace the three main problems.

## 9. Starter and Personalized Programs

### Starter plan

Before additional quantitative assessments are complete, show no more than two
activities:

1. rising or transfer practice at the safest current assistance level;
2. short movement or walking intervals alternated with rest.

### Personalized plan

After sufficient inputs are complete, generate no more than four activities.
Keep the main movement pattern when possible and personalize:

- assistance and support;
- frequency;
- intensity or effort target;
- repetitions, duration, and rest;
- weekly progression or regression criteria;
- stop conditions;
- substitution only when the original movement is unsafe or impossible.

Examples of substitutions include bridging or supported bed mobility for a
person who cannot yet stand safely, and seated or supported stepping for a
person who cannot yet complete household walking practice.

## 10. Value and Stop-Point Communication

The result must show measurable value without promising cure:

- less help required to rise or transfer;
- safer access to an important household destination;
- fewer falls or near falls;
- longer activity with shorter recovery;
- reduced caregiver assistance for selected tasks.

Paid physical therapy may be reduced or stopped when the person and family can
perform and progress the plan safely, meaningful function is improving or
stable, and further professional visits are no longer changing the plan.

## 11. Contextual Article Linking

Do not show four large article cards together.

- Article 1 appears inside the expandable frailty condition explanation.
- Article 2 appears in relevant assessment panels.
- Article 3 appears near the opening profile rationale.
- Article 4 appears near the personalized program and consult decision.

Use a small `ความรู้เพิ่มเติม` treatment consistent with the approved Stroke
plan. Links open in a new tab. The app state remains available when the user
returns. Articles link back to the main app and never preselect a Team 1 goal or
Team 4 plan.

Article URLs should be stored as shared content data rather than repeated as
hard-coded text in several plan pages.

## 12. Safety and Clinical Boundaries

- Use `พบสัญญาณ` or `เข้าได้กับ` rather than diagnosing frailty from self-report.
- A medically unstable user or a user with a red flag does not receive a normal
  exercise progression result.
- Do not reproduce licensed assessment instruments unless their use and wording
  are permitted.
- Do not prescribe medicine, supplements, laboratory testing, or nutrition
  treatment outside professional scope.
- Explain when physician, dietitian, pharmacist, occupational therapist,
  speech-language professional, nurse, or another professional may be needed.
- Use synthetic examples only during development and testing.
- Do not treat browser storage as a clinical record.

## 13. Evidence Foundation

Use primary or authoritative sources in article research and citation:

- WHO Integrated Care for Older People (ICOPE), second edition, 2025.
- British Geriatrics Society, Introduction to Frailty and Fit for Frailty.
- NICE guidance on comprehensive geriatric assessment and transitions of care.
- CDC STEADI for fall screening, gait, strength, balance, and home-risk logic.
- Original or validated sources for Fried phenotype, sarcopenia, functional
  measures, and Thai-language validation when those details are used.

The existing SWU 505-506 functional assessment work can inform the clinical
content map, but public claims and cutoffs must be checked against the cited
source before publication.

## 14. Analytics Boundary

Campaign and article-entry parameters may be captured for aggregate marketing
analysis. They must not change Team 1 presentation, clinical questions, scoring,
main-plan selection, or personalized recommendations.

No name, phone number, address, or health information is required merely to
read an article or receive the initial result. Contact data is requested only
at the existing detailed-plan or consultation step.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria

The design is ready for implementation when the user confirms:

- the F01 patient-facing name;
- the three main problems;
- the four article titles and distinct roles;
- the contextual link locations;
- the starter-plan limit of two activities;
- the personalized-plan limit of four activities;
- the transparent paid-care stop point;
- the rule that advertisements never alter the app result.

The implementation is accepted when:

- all four articles render correctly on mobile and desktop;
- each article is discoverable through the normal blog system;
- Team 4 links to the correct article in the correct context;
- opening and returning from an article does not erase the result state;
- article CTAs return to the main app without preselecting a path;
- the Team 4 plan displays exactly three primary problems and at most two
  modifiers;
- red-flag behavior prevents unsafe normal-plan progression;
- content, link, responsive-layout, and decision-rule checks pass.

## 16. Scope Boundary for This Design

This design defines the frailty knowledge cluster, the Team 4 aggregate-result
contract, and the first frailty main-plan profile. It does not finalize the full
Team 1-3 question set, scoring thresholds, the complete Team 4 catalogue, or the
final patient-facing plan number. Those items require a separate review against
the existing flows before they become implementation rules.
